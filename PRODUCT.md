# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Owners, managers and team leads who need technology built, set up or looked
after (software, a website, phones and VoIP, a network, email and devices,
IT support) and are deciding who to hire. Mostly small and mid-sized
businesses, since Wyzie is one too, but the copy never says so: nothing on
the site should make a larger company feel it is in the wrong place. They arrive with a project in
mind, usually from a referral, a search or one of Wyzie's own products, and
give the site a couple of minutes to answer one question: is this a real team
that ships and keeps things running?

Also: investors and prospective buyers sizing up the company. The site has to
feel like a modern, confident product in the first few seconds of scrolling.

Secondary: developers who found Wyzie through Wyzie Subs, Wyzie Lib or another
public project and want to see what else the same people made.

## Product Purpose

wyzie.io sells Wyzie LLC's work: custom web apps, websites and stores,
business phone systems (VoIP), networks and security, email and devices,
cloud and infrastructure, integrations, MVPs, and IT support and consulting. The proof is the work, so the
site shows everything Wyzie has built and runs under its name (Wyzie Subs and
its tools, Kilter, pitmaster.cc and the side projects), each with a real link.

Success is a visitor who looks at the work, believes it, and emails
hello@wyzie.io about their project.

## Brand Personality

**Exact, plain-spoken, proven, bright, and good in the hand.**

- **Exact**: sharp edges, aligned columns, one accent.
- **Plain-spoken**: short factual sentences. Say what a thing does and what it
  runs on. No hype words, no invented numbers, no exclamation marks.
- **Proven**: the work carries the argument. Real screenshots, real links, real
  figures only where they are verifiable.
- **Bright**: lit rather than dim. Night-blue surfaces, electric blue light,
  things that glow because something lights them. Futuristic through light
  and depth, never through costume.
- **Tactile**: the page answers the hand. Presses give and click, rows tick as
  the pointer crosses them, light follows the pointer, the lamp over the hero
  strikes on. Small, quick, and never in the way of reading.

The reference is kilter.work (Kilter is a Wyzie LLC product): flat near-black
neutral surfaces, hairline rules, semibold title-case headlines set tight,
real product views instead of icon cards, ruled rows instead of card grids.

## Anti-references

- **The site as it was, minus its light**: violet glow, gradient text,
  rotating hero words, a stats band with animated counters, four icon cards.
  The light bar and the pointer light were the good part and came back
  (2026-09-27, at the owner's request), rebuilt in the new palette.
- AI-template tells: a small uppercase eyebrow over every heading, numbered
  "01 / 02 / 03" rows, a standfirst that restates the headline, "X, not Y"
  sentences, alliterative value titles ("Built With Intent").
- Generic SaaS and agency templates: gradient heroes, big-number stat strips,
  grids of identical icon-and-heading cards, "Trusted by millions" badges with a
  pulsing dot, glassmorphism.
- Monospace tags as a costume for "technical".

## Design Principles

1. **Show the work, not adjectives.** Every claim about skill is backed by a
   project a visitor can open.
2. **Only true numbers.** A figure appears only if it can be traced to a system
   or a document. When in doubt, leave it out.
3. **Rows before cards.** Lists are ruled rows; a framed panel is reserved for a
   real view of a product.
4. **One blue.** Wyzie blue carries the actions and one committed moment on the
   page. Everything else is neutral.
5. **Light is the motif.** One lamp over the hero: it strikes on like a tube
   (throwing sparks), casts a glare, lights a dotted wall behind it, and dust
   falls slowly through the beam, untouched by the pointer. L or its pull
   cord flips the switch. The pointer is a second, small light: panels flare
   where it is nearest their edge, rows warm under it, the wall shows around
   it, and the hero turns toward it. The Live Now panel stands in 3D over a
   lit plate. Scrolling is felt: a beam fills under the header, frames tip up
   and catch a glare, rules light as they pass. Everything that glows is lit
   by something.
6. **Every press answers.** Controls give under the finger and make a short,
   synthesized sound; opening, closing and copying each have their own. One
   switch in the header mutes all of it, and the choice is kept.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Text on every surface at least 4.5:1. Visible focus ring on every
control. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes all travel (the lamp is simply on, the dust
still, the pointer light off); content never depends on JavaScript to become
visible. Sound never carries information on its own. Keyboard reachable throughout, rem-based sizing
so text scaling moves the layout. Under forced colors the light show steps
aside; `prefers-contrast: more` firms the rules and secondary text.
